## The High Horse Society (HHS)

Social media can be good. The High Horse Society is an experiment in building a healthier, more meaningful way to interact online. To achieve this, every post and response must meet a baseline of quality and effort. Instead of likes or upvotes, posts are filtered by their craftsmanship and substance.

---

### 1. Only Good Posts Allowed

Most bad posts online add noise rather than insight. On HHS, all contributions must meet the following standards:

* **Word count:**
  Posts must be at least **1000 words**, and replies at least **250 words**. Short posts tend to lack depth and require less effort to create than to respond to.

* **Information density:**
  Verbosity without substance doesn’t count. Each essay must meet a minimum level of informational richness. For now, this will be measured via an **LLM-assisted rubric**, but future versions will use more advanced textual metrics.

* **Logical soundness:**
  Arguments must hold together logically. This will also be evaluated using an LLM in the early stages.

* **Valid sourcing:**
  Claims should be grounded in credible data. Over time, source quality will be ranked and required according to this hierarchy:

  1. Meta-studies
  2. Direct data or primary studies
  3. Summaries or secondary sources

  Sources may be critiqued; a successfully challenged source becomes void.

---

### 2. Chronological Feed Only

No algorithms. No popularity contests. Posts appear in **reverse chronological order**, encouraging genuine reflection over vanity. Future updates may allow optional “journal” groupings, but the core feed will always remain chronological.

---

### 3. Strict Daily Posting Limits

Each member may post **one essay per day** by default. This prevents spam and encourages thoughtful participation. Additional posting privileges may be earned through meaningful engagement (details to come).

---

### 4. Contestation and Retraction

All essays and critiques are open to challenge.

* If a **logical error** is identified in a critique, the essay must be retracted and corrected before resubmission.
* If a **source is contested**, two outcomes are possible:

  1. **Higher-tier source:** The essay must retract.
  2. **Equal-tier source:** The essay is marked **contested**—it remains visible but can be filtered out.

To remove the contested tag, the author must either integrate the critique’s new evidence or demonstrate flaws in the opposing source through standard critique methods.

Successful retraction attempts grant an **extra essay posting token**. A published essay—one that has withstood contestation—also grants an additional token.
A global leaderboard tracking earned tokens and their sources (critiques vs. original essays) will serve as the record of HHS “winners.”
